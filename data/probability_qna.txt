Q: What is a probability distribution?
A: A probability distribution assigns likelihoods to outcomes in a sample space.

Q: What is the law of total probability?
A: The law of total probability provides a way to find the probability of an event by considering a set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive events.

Q: What is Bayes' rule?
A: Bayes' rule is a mathematical formula for determining conditional probability. It describes how to update the probability of a hypothesis based on new evidence.

Q: What is a random variable?
A: A random variable is a variable whose value is a numerical outcome of a random phenomenon.

Q: What is the difference between expectation and variance?
A: Expectation is the average value of a random variable, while variance measures the spread or dispersion of the variable around its mean.

Q: What is the Central Limit Theorem?
A: The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of the sum (or average) of a large number of independent, identically distributed random variables will be approximately normal, regardless of the underlying distribution.

Q: What are the three axioms of probability?
A: 1. The probability of an event is a non-negative real number. 2. The probability of the entire sample space is 1. 3. The probability of the union of mutually exclusive events is the sum of their individual probabilities.

Q: What is conditional probability?
A: Conditional probability is the probability of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred.

Q: What are some common probability distributions?
A: Common probability distributions include the Normal (Gaussian), Uniform, Exponential, and Beta distributions, in addition to the Bernoulli, Binomial, and Poisson distributions.

Q: What is a Markov chain?
A: A Markov chain is a stochastic model describing a sequence of possible events in which the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event.

Q: What is the Markov property?
A: The Markov property, also known as the "memoryless" property, states that the future state of a process depends only on the current state, not on the sequence of events that preceded it.

Q: What is entropy in information theory?
A: In information theory, entropy is a measure of the uncertainty or randomness in a random variable.

Q: What is Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence?
A: KL divergence is a measure of how one probability distribution is different from a second, reference probability distribution.

Q: What is mutual information?
A: Mutual information is a measure of the mutual dependence between two random variables. It quantifies the "amount of information" obtained about one random variable by observing the other.
